## Field Notes

The biggest mistake is buying for capacity on paper instead of route reality. A truck that looks perfect in export specs may be too long for tight city streets, too heavy for local bridge limits, or too expensive to run in stop-and-go traffic. Another frequent issue is over-specifying refrigeration. If the cargo is chilled, not frozen, a smaller and more efficient cooling system often lowers fuel use and maintenance costs.

For overseas buyers importing special vehicles from China, the value of a box truck is not in the brochure specs but in how it performs in daily business. In Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and parts of Latin America, box trucks are used for fast-moving consumer goods, bottled water, appliances, furniture, pharmaceuticals, and municipal supply runs. A refrigerated box truck is especially common where cold-chain logistics are still developing and operators need a practical, lower-cost entry point.
